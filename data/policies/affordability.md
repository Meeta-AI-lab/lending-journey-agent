# How we check affordability
Document: POL-AFF | Version 1.0 | Synthetic policy for a portfolio simulation. Not the policy of any real bank.

## Why we check whether a loan is affordable
We must make sure any loan we offer is one you can repay comfortably without falling into financial difficulty. Responsible lending rules require this, and it protects you as well as us.

## How we work out your disposable income
We start with your verified monthly take-home income from your bank transactions. We then subtract your regular committed outgoings, such as rent or mortgage, existing credit repayments, and household bills. We also subtract an allowance for everyday living costs. What remains is your disposable income.

## The living-cost allowance used in affordability checks
Our living-cost allowance is £600 a month for the applicant, plus £250 a month for each dependant. We use a fixed allowance rather than your actual spending on things like food and shopping, so that the affordability check is consistent and fair for every customer.

## Stress testing your loan repayment
Your loan has a fixed interest rate, but as a safety margin we check whether the repayment would still be affordable at a rate 3 percentage points higher than the rate offered. This stress test helps make sure the loan stays affordable if your circumstances change.

## The affordability limit for a loan
The stress-tested monthly repayment must be no more than 40% of your disposable income. If the amount you asked for is above this limit, we will tell you the largest amount we could offer instead.

## Using Open Banking to check affordability
With your permission, we use Open Banking to read your recent bank transactions. This lets us verify your income and outgoings accurately rather than relying on estimates. Our Open Banking and data use policy explains this in more detail.
